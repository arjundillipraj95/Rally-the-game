## Armies: places, holding and recruits

Each role recruits only two soldier types, and every pair of roles shares one type, so a mixed team has everything and a stacked team is missing something.

|  | Base places | Castle held | Village held | Recruits | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Defender | 7 | +5 | +3 | Spearmen, archers | A spear line that stops cavalry |
| Attacker | 5 | +3 | +2 | Archers, stone throwers | Siege |
| Raider | 3 | +3 | +2 | Swordsmen, cavalry | Riders that fall back to swordsmen |

- **Holding is flat by role**, per captain (today it is one pot split evenly). A team of three Defenders gets a far bigger army than three Raiders for the same map; the smaller team must win with strategy (stones punish big crowds, Raiders outscore elsewhere). To verify in team-composition tests; tune if the big army wins more than about 60%.
- **Recruit slots:** footmen, archers and swordsmen take 1 place; a mounted swordsman takes 2; a stone thrower takes 5.
- **The role applies to the whole army**: a Defender's archers get his extra health and lower damage.

- **Stone throwers have no cap:** fielding three takes holding a lot of the map, which is the limit.
- **The glacier is captains only:** soldiers and stone throwers can't hit it. Every captain hit counts, including abilities and ults, at normal range (the Attacker shoots it from his usual distance). Its health becomes a damage pool, retuned in the numbers pass: about 3 minutes for one captain, about 1 minute for three focusing. The war chest can be hit by anyone, and its health gets retuned for roles in the numbers pass.
- **Raider's 3 places punch above their weight:** each cavalryman is two bodies (horse, then swordsman), plus the Raider captain, enough to make the jungle worth it. If camps prove too hard, tune the camps, not his base.
